# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary users are people using a browser-based chat app to keep up with private conversations, group chats, shared media, files, stories, and lightweight audio/video calling. They are usually in a social, back-and-forth workflow where speed, clarity, and trust matter more than novelty. Secondary surfaces include the public home and auth flow, but the authenticated chat workspace is the product's center of gravity.

## Product Purpose

This product is a rich media messenger: a web chat experience where users can start conversations, stay aware of friends, exchange text, images, video, audio, documents, captions, stories, and audio/video calls without the interface getting in the way. Success means conversations feel immediate, media feels native to the chat, and new or empty accounts always have a clear next step.

## Positioning

A playful social messenger that treats media, stories, files, and calls as first-class parts of everyday conversation.

## Brand Personality

Playful, social, and approachable. The UI should feel friendly and alive around people, stories, media, and presence, while staying clear enough for fast repeated chat workflows. Motion and color can add warmth, but they should support state, attention, and feedback rather than decorate every surface.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product feel like a generic SaaS dashboard, a cold enterprise inbox, or an over-decorated social feed. Avoid heavy gradients, decorative glass panels, oversized marketing-style sections inside the app, and unfamiliar controls for standard chat actions. Avoid empty states that only say nothing exists; they should help users start a chat, find friends, or add a story.

## Design Principles

Media is part of the message, not an attachment afterthought. Images, video, audio, documents, captions, and previews should have layouts that match their actual type and preserve room for timestamps and status.

Every social state needs clear feedback. Sending, typing, calling, online presence, story upload, editing, and deletion should communicate what is happening without making the user wait or guess.

Keep chat familiar, then add personality at the edges. Core actions should use recognizable messenger patterns; playful touches belong in stories, profile surfaces, empty states, transitions, and media affordances.

Design for fresh accounts as carefully as active ones. A new user with no conversations still needs a polished path into search, friends, story discovery, and starting the first chat.

Prefer calm density over decorative space. The app should fit real conversations, files, and side panels comfortably while keeping text readable and controls easy to hit.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target accessible product UI defaults: readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic buttons for chat actions, non-color-only states, and reduced-motion fallbacks for typing, call, story, and media animations. Media controls should have labels, disabled states should be obvious, and avatar fallbacks should avoid empty image sources.
